## 1. Liability for false positives and damaging remediation; how EULAs limit liability and where limits fail (Brazil CDC, EU consumer law / new Product Liability Directive, US FTC Act)

### Takeaway
Two distinct liability families exist: (a) **deception** (scareware-style exaggerated findings, fake scans, dark-pattern subscriptions), which is what regulators and class actions have actually punished in the consumer cleaner/security category; and (b) **defect/damage** (a bad update or false positive that breaks systems or deletes data), where B2B liability caps have mostly held in the US but are **void or sharply limited against consumers in Brazil and the EU**, and where the EU's new Product Liability Directive makes software a "product" with strict liability for consumer data loss for products placed on the market from 9 Dec 2026.

### Cited Findings

- Securities class action (theory: false/misleading statements about testing and QA) dismissed by Judge Robert Pitman (W.D. Tex.) on 12 Jan 2026 for failure to plead intent to defraud; the court said corporate mismanagement alone does not create a securities claim — [Reuters, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/crowdstrike-defeats-shareholder-lawsuit-over-huge-software-outage-2026-01-14/); [Computer Weekly, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366637266/Texas-judge-throws-out-second-lawsuit-over-CrowdStrike-outage)
- Shareholders dropped the consolidated derivative suit on 14 Apr 2026 after no appeal of the class-action dismissal — [Bloomberg Law, 14 Apr 2026](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/securities-law/crowdstrike-shareholders-drop-board-suit-over-massive-it-outage)
- Airline-passenger class actions (negligence, public nuisance) were dismissed in June 2025 on Airline Deregulation Act preemption grounds — [Computer Weekly, 14 Jan 2026](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366637266/Texas-judge-throws-out-second-lawsuit-over-CrowdStrike-outage). A secondary source (analysis site summarising CrowdStrike's 10-K; lower reliability) reports the Fifth Circuit affirmed on 20 May 2026 and that Delta is "the one live claim"; it also reports net incident costs of US$60.1M (FY2025) and US$117.7M (FY2026) — [MoatExplorer, 28 Sep 2026](https://moatexplorer.com/crowdstrike/the-moat/the-test-of-july-19/four-sets-of-lawsuits-three-dismissed/)
- Delta v. CrowdStrike (Fulton County Superior Court, No. 24CV013621; Delta claims >US$500M): on 16 May 2025 Judge Kelly Lee Ellerbe let **gross negligence** and **computer trespass** claims proceed and dismissed most fraud claims. Delta alleged the update was deployed "without minimal testing [or] routine quality and assurance" and "without staged deployments", and without its consent — [CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/security/2025/5-things-to-watch-in-delta-s-lawsuit-against-crowdstrike); [Reuters, 19 May 2025](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/boards-policy-regulation/delta-can-sue-crowdstrike-over-computer-outage-that-caused-7000-canceled-flights-2025-05-19/)
- The Delta–CrowdStrike contract capped liability and excluded consequential damages, **but the caps did not apply to gross negligence or willful misconduct** (SSA §9.3) — [Court order, 16 May 2025 (PDF)](https://johnbandler.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/2025-05-16-Order-Delta-v-Crowdstrike-GA-Fulton-Co.pdf). CrowdStrike's counsel says remaining liability will be "contractually capped in the single-digit-millions" — [CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/security/2025/5-things-to-watch-in-delta-s-lawsuit-against-crowdstrike)
- Legal commentary: the ruling shows a limitation-of-liability clause "may not be the final word" where a duty exists independently of the contract (gross negligence, fraud, statutory violations) — [Lucia Law, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.lucialaw.com/post/delta-airlines-v-crowdstrike-when-saas-liability-caps-may-not-be-the-ceiling)

**False-positive / damaging-remediation incidents (pattern is old and recurring)**
- McAfee DAT 5958 (21 Apr 2010) flagged `svchost.exe` on Windows XP SP3 as W32/Wecorl.a and deleted it, causing reboot loops — [CISA alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2010/04/21/mcafee-dat-5958-issues); [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2010/04/broken-mcafee-dat-update-cripples-windows-workstations/)
- Sophos (Sept 2012) detected its own updater (and others such as Adobe Flash) as "Shh/Updater-B"; customers whose policy was "delete" rather than "quarantine" were in a much worse position — [404 Tech Support](https://www.404techsupport.com/2012/09/23/sophos-false-positive-quarantines-itself-with-shhupdater-b-detection/)
- Webroot (24 Apr 2017): a two-hour bad classification quarantined legitimate Windows files and flagged Facebook as phishing; one MSP said it was considering legal action — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/406549/webroot-deletes-windows-files-and-causes-serious-problems-for-users.html)
- Microsoft Defender ASR rule (13 Jan 2023) deleted application shortcuts (.lnk) across managed devices; Microsoft's fix **did not restore deleted files** — devices in "audit" mode were unaffected, only "block" mode — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/buggy-microsoft-defender-asr-rule-deletes-windows-app-shortcuts/); [Microsoft Q&A on GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/MDE-PowerBI-Templates/blob/refs/heads/master/ASR_scripts/ASR_rule_Block_Win32_API_calls_from_Office_Macro_issue_Q&A.md)
- A long-running catalogue lists dozens of similar update/false-positive failures (AVG, Avira, Symantec, Kaspersky, Bitdefender, Malwarebytes Apr 2013, including Mac-side incidents such as "Symantec false alert floors Macs" 2005 and McAfee revoking its Mac signing key 2013) — [attrition.org Auto Fail](https://attrition.org/errata/autofail/)
- AV-Comparatives: a false positive on a system file can render a machine unusable; one product even detected its own updater — [AV-Comparatives, 3 Sep 2018](https://av-comparatives.org/spotlight-on-security-the-problem-with-false-alarms/)

**Deception cases against cleaner / "repair" / security vendors (US)**
- MacKeeper: *Yencha v. ZeoBIT LLC* (W.D. Pa., No. 14-cv-00578, filed May 2014). Allegation: the free trial scan was designed to "invariably and ominously report" that the Mac needed repair regardless of actual condition, to sell the US$39.95 licence. Claims: breach of contract, unjust enrichment, Pennsylvania consumer protection/fraud. Settled for a **US$2,000,000** fund (≈513,000 class members; up to US$39.95 each; US$660,000 in fees), final approval Oct/Nov 2015; no admission, no injunctive relief — [Settlement notice (PDF)](https://yenchasoftwaresettlement.com/Portals/0/Documents/Website%20Notice_v1.pdf); [Final approval order (PDF)](https://yenchasoftwaresettlement.com/Portals/0/Documents/Yencha-ZeoBIT%20DKT%2055%20Final%20Approval%20Order.pdf); [Top Class Actions](https://topclassactions.com/lawsuit-settlements/closed-settlements/mackeeper-class-action-settlement/); [Truth in Advertising](https://truthinadvertising.org/class-action/mackeeper/)
- FTC v. Restoro/Reimage (Mar 2024): **US$26M** under FTC Act §5 and the Telemarketing Sales Rule. FTC test machines with no issues "revealed" hundreds of problems ("PC Privacy issues", "Junk files", "Broken Registry issues"). The order permanently bans misrepresenting "that a security or performance issue has been detected on a consumer's Electronic Device" — [FTC stipulated order (PDF)](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Restoro.SignedOrder.pdf); [BleepingComputer, 14 Mar 2024](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/tech-support-firms-restoro-reimage-fined-26-million-for-scare-tactics/). Refunds of US$25.5M to 736,375 consumers began 13 Mar 2025 — [BleepingComputer, 10 Mar 2025](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ftc-will-send-255-million-to-victims-of-tech-support-scams/)
- FTC v. Office Depot / Support.com (Mar 2019): **US$35M** (US$25M + US$10M); "PC Health Check" reported "malware symptoms" based on four survey questions rather than the scan — [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2019/03/office-depot-tricked-people-into-buying-pc-support-with-fake-virus-scans/); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/article/technology/office-depot-supportcom-pay-35-million-to-settle-ftc-allegations-idUSKCN1R82G9/)
- FTC + Florida v. PC Cleaner Inc., Boost Software et al. (Nov 2014; >US$120M consumer loss alleged): free trial scan "always identifies numerous errors"; settlements 2016 including Texas DTPA — [Ars Technica, 19 Nov 2014](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2014/11/ftc-windows-tech-support-scams-took-another-120-million-from-pc-users/); [Texas AG, 14 Jul 2016](https://www.oag.state.tx.us/news/releases/texas-ag-files-settlement-against-florida-company-nationwide-tech-support-scam)
- FTC v. Avast (2024): **US$16.5M** and a ban on selling browsing data for advertising; a privacy/security product misrepresented how it shared user browsing data via subsidiary Jumpshot (Aug 2014–Jan 2020) — [FTC Decision & Order (PDF)](https://search.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/D%26O-Avast.pdf)
- Subscriptions: the FTC "Click-to-Cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025; the FTC restarted rulemaking (draft ANPRM sent to OIRA 30 Jan 2026; ANPRM issued March 2026). **ROSCA and FTC Act §5 remain fully enforceable** (clear disclosure of terms, express informed consent, simple cancellation) — [Gibson Dunn, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/ftc-restarts-negative-option-rulemaking-after-eighth-circuit-vacatur-enforcement-under-rosca-continues/); [BCLP, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/im-not-dead-yet-is-the-demise-of-the-ftcs-click-to-cancel-exaggerated.html); [Jones Day, May 2026](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2026/05/ftc-revives-clicktocancel-rule-new-risks-for-subscription-businesses)

**Risk in the other direction: being sued for what you flag**
- *Enigma Software v. Malwarebytes* (9th Cir., 2 Jun 2023): when a security company labels a competitor's software "malicious" or a "threat", that is "more a statement of objective fact than a non-actionable opinion" and can support a Lanham Act false-advertising claim; **"potentially unwanted" was held too unspecific to be actionable** — [Ninth Circuit opinion (PDF)](https://cdn.ca9.uscourts.gov/datastore/opinions/2023/06/02/21-16466.pdf). On remand the district court denied Malwarebytes' renewed motion to dismiss on 6 Jun 2024 — [Justia docket](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5:2017cv02915/312023/189/)

**Brazil — Código de Defesa do Consumidor (CDC) and Lei do Software**
- CDC art. 51, I: clauses that "impossibilitem, exonerem ou atenuem a responsabilidade do fornecedor por vícios de qualquer natureza" are **null**; limitation is possible only where the consumer is a legal entity, "em situações justificáveis". Art. 51 also voids compulsory arbitration (VII) and clauses limiting access to the courts (XVII) — [CDC text, Procon-SP edition 2025 (PDF)](https://www.procon.sp.gov.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/CDC_2025.pdf)
- CDC art. 25 forbids clauses that prevent, exonerate or attenuate the duty to indemnify; limiting clauses must be drafted with emphasis (art. 54 §4) — [Merovian, 15 Oct 2026 (legal explainer; secondary)](https://merovian.app/blog/contratos-saas-clausulas-essenciais)
- Brazilian doctrine applying CDC arts. 12 and 14 to the CrowdStrike scenario: a defective update that compromises system operation is a product/service defect; supplier liability is **strict (objetiva)**, based on enterprise risk, and joint and several across the supply chain (art. 7, sole para.); developers are expected to have enhanced pre-deployment testing, automated rollback and recovery plans — [Migalhas, Laís Bergstein, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.migalhas.com.br/coluna/migalhas-de-responsabilidade-civil/443574/responsabilidade-civil-por-falhas-em-softwares-caso-crowdstrike)
- Outside consumer relations (B2B under the Civil Code), limitation and exoneration clauses are generally valid (caps tied to contract value, exclusion of lost profits) — [Migalhas, 4 Apr 2025](https://www.migalhas.com.br/depeso/427638/clausulas-de-limitacao-de-responsabilidade-nos-contratos-de-software); [Almeida Advogados](https://almeidalaw.com.br/limitacao-da-responsabilidade-nos-contratos-de-software/)
- Lei 9.609/1998 art. 10 §1 II voids licence clauses that exempt a party from liability for third-party actions arising from "vícios, defeitos ou violação de direitos de autor" — [Almeida Advogados](https://almeidalaw.com.br/limitacao-da-responsabilidade-nos-contratos-de-software/)
- LGPD art. 42: controller/operator must repair damage caused by violating data-protection law; a vendor–customer contract cannot bind the data subject — [Merovian, 15 Oct 2026](https://merovian.app/blog/contratos-saas-clausulas-essenciais)

**EU — new Product Liability Directive (EU) 2024/2853**
- Applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 8 Dec 2026 (i.e. from 9 Dec 2026); old Directive 85/374/EEC repealed from 9 Dec 2026 but still governs older products. Does **not** apply to free and open-source software developed or supplied outside a commercial activity — [EUR-Lex consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02024L2853-20241118)
- Software (all types, incl. apps and AI) is a "product"; manufacturer is liable for defects arising from updates/upgrades under its control; when the manufacturer is outside the EU "there is always a liable person in the EU" — [EUR-Lex summary](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/EN/legal-content/summary/defective-products-liability.html)
- Compensable damage (Art. 6) includes "destruction or corruption of data that are not used for professional purposes", plus property damage (except property used exclusively professionally) and personal injury incl. medically recognised psychological harm — [EUR-Lex consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02024L2853-20241118)
- Defectiveness factors (Art. 7) include "safety-relevant cybersecurity requirements" and, "in the case of a product whose very purpose is to prevent damage, any failure of the product to fulfil that purpose" — [EUR-Lex consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02024L2853-20241118)
- The "defect arose later" defence is unavailable where the defect is due to software updates, or **a lack of software updates necessary to maintain safety**, within the manufacturer's control (Art. 11(2)) — [EUR-Lex consolidated text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX%3A02024L2853-20241118)
- The PLD itself creates no duty to update (Recital 51); that duty comes from the CRA. Business-data loss is outside the PLD and falls back on contract law — [Nebrino guide, 20 Oct 2026](https://nebrino.de/en/guides/product-liability-software); [Dreyfus, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/09/25/defective-software-how-does-directive-eu-2024-2853-reshape-manufacturers-product-liability/)
- Not every bug is a "defect": the test remains the safety the public is entitled to expect — [Dreyfus, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.dreyfus.fr/en/2026/09/25/defective-software-how-does-directive-eu-2024-2853-reshape-manufacturers-product-liability/); US-side summary — [American Bar Association, 5 Nov 2026](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/litigation/resources/newsletters/mass-torts/europe-rewrites-product-liability-playbook-global-manufacturers-directive-2024-2853/)

**EU — consumer contract law for digital content**
- Directive (EU) 2019/770 (applicable since 1 Jan 2022) gives consumers remedies for faulty digital content, including software: bring into conformity, then price reduction or termination with refund — [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/business-and-industry/contract-rules/digital-contracts/digital-contract-rules_en)

### Inferences
- A tool that kills processes, quarantines files, disables launch items and revokes permissions sits squarely in the PLD's new "destruction or corruption of non-professional data" head of damage. A destructive false positive on an EU consumer's Mac after 9 Dec 2026 is a plausible strict-liability claim; a liability cap in the EULA will not be a reliable defence against consumers in the EU or Brazil. **[LAWYER-EU] [LAWYER-BR]**
- The PLD factor "product whose very purpose is to prevent damage" cuts both ways: *missed* detections could be argued as defectiveness too. Marketing claims ("removes all malware", "protects your Mac") therefore expand exposure; narrow, accurate claims ("inspects persistence items and shows you what they are") reduce it.
- The enforcement record shows the highest-probability legal risk for a small vendor is **marketing/UX deception**, not a damages suit: scan results that look alarming on a clean machine, paywalls after a scary scan, and hard-to-cancel subscriptions are the exact fact patterns in MacKeeper, Restoro, Office Depot and PC Cleaner.
- Design choices that double as legal mitigation, drawn from the incidents: default to **report/audit mode**, make every remediation **reversible** (quarantine not delete; Sophos and Defender cases), keep a built-in undo log, require explicit per-item consent, allowlist Apple/OS components, and stage rollouts (the absence of staged deployment is a core pleaded fact in Delta's gross-negligence claim).
- Labelling third-party software: prefer descriptive, verifiable facts ("unsigned", "not notarized", "launches at login", "developer unknown") and "potentially unwanted" style language over "malicious"/"threat" unless there is solid evidence (Enigma). **[LAWYER-US]**
- EULA still matters for B2B users, for US consumers (where disclaimers and caps are generally more enforceable, subject to state law and gross-negligence carve-outs), and for setting expectations; it should carve consumer-mandatory rights out explicitly rather than pretend to exclude them.

### Gaps
- No documented case found of a **Mac cleaner app being sued for deleting user data** (CleanMyMac, etc.); forum anecdotes exist but I found no court filing or reliable report in this session.
- Did not verify the PLD's article barring contractual exclusion of liability (believed to be Art. 15) or the exact chain of liable EU operators (importer → authorised representative → fulfilment service provider → online platform) against the text; the EUR-Lex summary only states that an EU-based liable person always exists. For a direct-download seller with no EU importer, who that "liable person" is (and whether a merchant of record could be treated as distributor) is unresolved. **[LAWYER-EU]**
- Member-state transposition status of the PLD as of Nov 2026 not checked.
- CDC art. 49 (7-day withdrawal for distance purchases) and EU 14-day withdrawal / waiver on download were not sourced from an article-level excerpt in this session; the full CDC text is in the Procon-SP PDF above. **[LAWYER-BR] [LAWYER-EU]**
- Whether Brazilian courts would assert jurisdiction and apply the CDC to foreign consumers, and how foreign consumers would enforce against a Brazilian SLU, was not researched. **[LAWYER-BR]**
- Final outcome/trial date of Delta v. CrowdStrike: none found as of Nov 2026.
- Current status of Enigma v. Malwarebytes after June 2024 not found.

## 2. Regulatory duties for software sold in the EU: Cyber Resilience Act (scope, timeline, small-vendor obligations, vulnerability handling/reporting), NIS2 spillovers, Brazil-specific regulation

### Takeaway
The CRA already binds the product in one respect: **since 11 Sep 2026 manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents (24 h / 72 h / 14 days) for any product with digital elements available in the EU**. Full obligations (risk assessment, technical file, CE marking, declared support period, free security updates) apply from **11 Dec 2027**. Software "that searches for, removes, or quarantines malicious software" is an **Important Product, Class I**, which blocks pure self-assessment unless harmonised standards are applied in full (or the product is FOSS with public technical documentation). Brazil has no CRA-equivalent product law yet.

### Cited Findings

**Timeline (all dates from official sources)**
- Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 entered into force 10 Dec 2024; Chapter IV (notified bodies) applies from 11 Jun 2026; **Art. 14 reporting from 11 Sep 2026**; full application **11 Dec 2027** — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary)
- Products placed on the market before 11 Dec 2027 are subject to the main requirements only if substantially modified after that date, **but reporting obligations apply to all products made available in the EU, including those already on the market** — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary); [European Commission news, 11 Sep 2026](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/safer-and-more-secure-digital-products-2026-09-11_en)
- 27 Jul 2026: Commission published its first (non-binding) implementation guidance, C(2026) 5252; 11 Dec 2026: Member States should have enough notified bodies; 30 Oct 2027 indicative date for further standards/supporting material — [Slovak NSA CRA timeline](https://cra.nbu.gov.sk/en/getting-started/dates/); [Arthur Cox, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/countdown-to-compliance-the-cyber-resilience-act-reporting-deadline-has-arrived/)
- The Commission guidance contains 67 practical examples aimed at microenterprises and SMEs — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-new-guidance-support-timely-cyber-resilience-act-implementation)

**Scope**
- Standalone software (apps, desktop programs) that is downloaded and runs on the user's system is in scope when supplied in the course of a commercial activity; it is "placed on the market" when first offered for distribution/use in the EU — [Commission guidance as reproduced by ORC WG](https://cra.orcwg.org/official-guidance/)
- Annex III, Class I, item 4: "Software that searches for, removes, or quarantines malicious software" — [EUR-Lex, Regulation 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EL-EN/TXT/?from=EN&uri=CELEX%3A32024R2847)
- Technical description in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2392: software "typically referred to as antivirus or antimalware, that detect or search for malicious software or code on devices, or remove or quarantine such software or code"; "includes but is not limited to software that detects or searches for malicious software in real-time **or manually**, rootkit detection and rescue disks" — [Cybiq (quoting the implementing regulation; secondary)](https://cybiq.eu/cra-class-anti-malware.html)
- Classification turns on **core functionality** (Art. 7(1)). The Commission's own example: an antivirus with an added disk-cleaning function is still classified by its antivirus core functionality — [Commission guidance via ORC WG](https://cra.orcwg.org/official-guidance/)

**Conformity route**
- Default-category products: self-assessment (module A). Class I: self-assessment **only** if harmonised standards, common specifications or an EU certification scheme (assurance ≥ "substantial") are applied in full; otherwise third-party assessment (module B+C or H) by a notified body — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary); [Commission guidance via ORC WG](https://cra.orcwg.org/official-guidance/)
- Exception (Art. 32(5)): Class I/II products that are **free and open-source software** may use self-assessment provided the technical documentation is made public — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary)
- Notified bodies must take the size of the undertaking into account, including for fees — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary)

**Manufacturer obligations (from 11 Dec 2027)**
- Cybersecurity risk assessment across the lifecycle; due diligence on third-party components; technical documentation kept for market-surveillance authorities; conformity assessment, EU declaration of conformity and CE marking; contact details; the **end date of the support period (month and year) stated at time of purchase** — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary)
- Vulnerabilities must be handled without delay during the support period and security updates provided free of charge — [Nebrino, 20 Oct 2026](https://nebrino.de/en/guides/product-liability-software)

**Reporting (in force since 11 Sep 2026)**
- Early warning within 24 h of becoming aware; full notification within 72 h; final report within 14 days after a corrective measure is available (actively exploited vulnerability) or within one month of the 72 h notification (severe incident). One submission via ENISA's Single Reporting Platform — [European Commission, CRA reporting](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)
- Affected users must also be informed; only *actively exploited* vulnerabilities and severe incidents are reportable — vulnerabilities fixed before exploitation stay in normal handling — [Arthur Cox, 8 Oct 2026](https://www.arthurcox.com/knowledge/countdown-to-compliance-the-cyber-resilience-act-reporting-deadline-has-arrived/); [Interlynk (vendor explainer)](https://www.interlynk.io/solutions/cyber-resilience-act)
- Micro and small enterprises cannot be fined for missing the **24-hour** early-warning deadline (Art. 64(10)); the 72 h and final-report deadlines are not covered by this exception; fines must consider company size — [European Commission, CRA summary](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-summary); [DataGuard](https://www.dataguard.com/cyber-resilience-act/timeline/)

**Non-EU manufacturer**
- A manufacturer **may** appoint an EU authorised representative by written mandate (optional); the representative cannot take over core duties such as the risk assessment. An importer is an EU-established person placing on the market a product bearing a non-EU party's name/trademark, and has its own verification duties — [EUR-Lex, Regulation 2024/2847](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EL-EN/TXT/?from=EN&uri=CELEX%3A32024R2847); [Clifford Chance (PDF)](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/PDFDocuments/the-eu-cyber-resilience-act.pdf)

**GDPR (for the vendor's own customer/telemetry data)**
- Art. 27: a non-EU controller offering goods/services to people in the EU must designate an EU representative in writing, unless processing is occasional, involves no large-scale special-category data and is unlikely to create risk; the representative does not absorb the controller's liability — [GDPR Art. 27 text](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/27/adopted?view=plain)
- Practitioner view: most online businesses selling regularly to EU customers do not meet the exemption; non-appointment exposes to fines up to €10M / 2% of turnover — [Prighter FAQ (representative-service vendor; interested party)](https://prighter.com/resources/faq/eu-gdpr-rep/)

**Brazil**
- No general cybersecurity law yet. PL 4752/2025 (Marco Legal da Cibersegurança) is still pending in the Senate's Science & Technology Committee; last status entry 3 Sep 2026 "matéria com a relatoria" — [Senado Federal](https://www25.senado.leg.br/web/atividade/materias/-/materia/170613/)

### Inferences
- A Mac tool that scans persistence items and can quarantine files / disable launch items very likely has the "core functionality" of Annex III Class I item 4 if it is positioned as detecting malware (note the implementing text explicitly includes *manual* scanning). Positioning as a general "startup-item inspector/diagnostic" might argue for the default class, but any "quarantine malicious software" feature and "security" marketing pulls it back in. This classification decision drives cost (notified body vs. self-assessment) and should be made with counsel before 2027. **[LAWYER-EU]**
- Open-sourcing has a concrete regulatory payoff: if the product qualifies as FOSS under the CRA, Art. 32(5) allows self-assessment with public technical documentation even in Class I. Open-sourcing only the helper while the app is proprietary probably does **not** make the *product* FOSS. **[LAWYER-EU]**
- Minimum CRA readiness now (already legally relevant since 11 Sep 2026): a monitored security contact, a written procedure to produce a 24 h/72 h report, an SRP account, and a way to notify users. From Dec 2027: a published support-period end date, which conflicts with "lifetime licence" marketing unless support is time-bounded.
- CRA compliance evidence will be read into PLD defectiveness (Art. 7 "safety-relevant cybersecurity requirements"), so the two regimes compound.

## 3. Stalkerware-adjacent ethics and safety: how a detection tool should warn at-risk users

### Takeaway
Consensus guidance from the Coalition Against Stalkerware, NNEDV Safety Net and EFF is clear and directly implementable: **never auto-remove, label stalkerware distinctly, warn that removal can alert the abuser and destroys evidence, give the user an explicit choice, and point to safety-planning resources**. As of the EFF/AV-Comparatives 2025 test, almost no commercial product does all of this — a differentiation opportunity as well as a duty of care.

### Cited Findings
- Coalition definition: software "made available directly to individuals, that enables a remote user to monitor the activities on another user's device without that user's consent and without explicit, persistent notification"; physical access/unlocking/installing with the password does not count as consent — [Coalition Against Stalkerware, information for tech companies](https://stopstalkerware.org/information-for-tech-companies/)
- Coalition detection criteria: apps marketed for covert spying; capable of tracking/monitoring/recording or remote control without continuous consent; exfiltrating sensitive data; hiding installation, resisting uninstall, using blank/random names — [Coalition Against Stalkerware](https://stopstalkerware.org/information-for-tech-companies/)
- Coalition detection-handling rules for vendors: do not allow stalkerware onto a safe list; notify via a separate channel (e.g. email) at install-time detection; "clearly notify users about the kind of threat found on the device that distinguishes it from ordinary malware"; "give the user a clear choice whether to remove the stalkerware or not", because "removal of stalkerware can be detected by the person who installed it" — [Coalition Against Stalkerware](https://stopstalkerware.org/information-for-tech-companies/)
- Survivor guidance: removal "may be detected by the abuser and could increase the abuse and harassment. Only attempt to remove stalkerware if you believe it is safe to do so"; make a safety plan **using a safer device**; deleting stalkerware deletes evidence; the abuser may reinstall — [Coalition Against Stalkerware, information for survivors (updated 3 Feb 2026)](https://stopstalkerware.org/stalkerware-detection-removal-and-prevention/)
- Security software "cannot serve as a one-size-fits-all 'solution'" and a lockdown suggestion "could also give a false sense of security" — [Coalition Against Stalkerware, information for media](https://stopstalkerware.org/information-for-media/)
- NNEDV Safety Net: if spyware is suspected, one of the safest things can be to keep using the device normally to avoid tipping off the abuser while collecting evidence; **do not change passwords on the compromised device** — use a separate, safer device; make a safety plan in case behaviour escalates — [NNEDV Safety Net, Spyware Evidence](https://www.techsafety.org/spyware-evidence)
- EFF / AV-Comparatives stalkerware test (published Nov 2025): products should label stalkerware clearly rather than as generic "PUA"; "automatic removal is inappropriate"; most tested products showed only "Malware detected"/"Threat detected"; most recommended immediate removal without explaining risk; **only Kaspersky warned about consequences of immediate uninstallation**; no product used an out-of-band notification channel, so on-device alerts may be seen by an abuser monitoring the screen — [EFF / AV-Comparatives report (PDF)](https://www.eff.org/files/2025/11/05/avc_stalkerware_2025.pdf)
- Coalition founded 19 Nov 2019 by EFF, Kaspersky, Avira, G Data, Malwarebytes, NNEDV, NortonLifeLock, Operation Safe Escape, WWP EN, Weisser Ring — [EFF press release](https://www.eff.org/press/releases/eff-antivirus-companies-and-human-rights-groups-launch-coalition-combat-stalkerware)
- Kaspersky on why this matters: on deletion "the operator (or abuser) will know immediately", with consequences ranging from conflict escalation to physical violence — [Kaspersky blog, 19 Nov 2019](https://www.kaspersky.com/blog/coalition-against-stalkerware/31307/)

### Inferences
- Responsible design for this product: (1) a distinct "monitoring/remote-access software" category separate from malware/PUA; (2) no one-click "fix all" that includes this category, no auto-remediation; (3) an interstitial before any action stating that removal may notify whoever installed it and will destroy evidence, with "export evidence report" and "leave in place" as first-class options; (4) links to local hotlines/Coalition resources and advice to seek help from a safer device; (5) quiet UI — avoid system notifications or emails to the Mac's own accounts that an observer could see; (6) a discreet/quick-exit path.
- Dual-use must be handled in both directions: legitimate MDM, parental-control and remote-support tools (Jamf, TeamViewer, employee monitoring) share technical traits with stalkerware. Describe capabilities neutrally ("can record screen; installed by X on date Y; hidden from Dock") rather than asserting intent — this also aligns with the Enigma defamation-style risk in section 1.
- The product itself has stalkerware-like privileges (root helper, visibility into processes). Avoid any remote-viewing/reporting-to-third-party feature and make uninstall trivial and complete, or the tool could be repurposed for control.
- Failing to warn where industry guidance is this explicit could be argued as a design defect/negligence if harm follows; following the Coalition guidance is cheap evidence of due care. **[LAWYER-BR] [LAWYER-EU]**

### Gaps
- Guidance is written mostly for phones; I found no macOS-specific stalkerware handling guidance from these bodies, and no reliable prevalence data for stalkerware on Macs.
- Coalition membership requirements/cost for a small vendor not found.
- No Brazil-specific survivor resource list was sourced (e.g. Ligue 180) — needs local sourcing before being hard-coded in the product.

## 4. Trust signals: audits/pentests, bug bounty/VDP, open-sourcing the privileged component, reproducible builds, transparency, AV-TEST / AV-Comparatives / AMTSO, and avoiding a PUP flag

### Takeaway
For a small macOS app with a root helper, the credible and affordable trust stack is: a **published third-party white-box audit focused on the helper/XPC/updater** (benchmarks: 6–30 person-days), an open-source helper, a vulnerability disclosure policy, and strict conformance to anti-PUP criteria (no exaggerated findings, no scare-to-pay, easy uninstall). AV-TEST/AV-Comparatives certification is built for full antivirus suites and is a poor fit; AMTSO membership is cheap (US$500/yr for the first two years) but optional.

### Cited Findings

**Published audit benchmarks (scope, team, effort)**
- X41 D-Sec, Mullvad VPN app (desktop incl. macOS daemon + mobile), white-box: **30 person-days**, 4 consultants, 23 Oct–28 Nov 2024; 6 vulnerabilities (3 high); focus included local privilege escalation via the daemon — [X41 public report, 10 Dec 2024 (PDF)](https://www.x41-dsec.de/static/reports/X41-Mullvad-Audit-Public-Report-2024-12-10.pdf)
- Cure53, Mullvad apps on five platforms: **20 days**, 6 testers, May–June 2020; 7 findings, max severity Medium. Cure53 called out the unprivileged-client → privileged-helper IPC as "one of the major risks", and praised .pkg installation as root (so the app bundle cannot be modified by a user) and the *absence* of an auto-updater as attack-surface reduction — [Cure53 report (PDF)](https://cure53.de/pentest-report_mullvad_2020_v2.pdf); [Mullvad audits folder on GitHub](https://github.com/mullvad/mullvadvpn-app/blob/main/audits/2020-06-12-cure53.md)

- Budget benchmark for this product (inference from published person-day counts × published day rates, not a quote): a focused white-box review of a small Swift app + privileged helper + updater is plausibly **6–15 person-days**. Elapsed time typically 2–6 weeks including reporting and retest. Obtain real quotes.
- Highest audit ROI scope, in order: XPC client validation in the helper, every root-executed file operation (TOCTOU/symlink), the update pipeline, installer/uninstaller, then the rule-update channel.
- A full bug-bounty platform is disproportionate pre-revenue; a `security.txt`, a published VDP with safe-harbour language and a modest discretionary reward matches what the CRA will require anyway (single point of contact + coordinated disclosure).
- AV-TEST/AV-Comparatives certification presumes on-access malware protection scored against large live sample sets; a persistence-inspection/diagnostic tool would score poorly on "protection" by design. Better third-party signals: published audit, open helper source, AMTSO membership (cheap), and proactively submitting builds to Malwarebytes/other vendors for allowlisting/reconsideration and to VirusTotal to catch false flags pre-release.
- The PUP criteria read like a product spec in the negative: no findings counter designed to alarm, no "X issues found — pay to fix", severity must be evidence-based, free tier must not be a scare funnel, and a one-click complete uninstaller (including the helper and LaunchDaemon) is mandatory.

## 5. Real-world vulnerabilities in Mac utilities' privileged helpers and the lessons

### Takeaway
The dominant failure is the same every time: a root helper that **accepts XPC requests from any local process** and exposes generic primitives (move/delete file, run command), or an updater/installer that verifies a file and then executes a copy an unprivileged user can still modify. The product being built *is* the highest-value local privilege-escalation target on the user's Mac, and a vulnerable helper left behind after uninstall remains exploitable.

### Cited Findings
- **CleanMyMac X (MacPaw), Cisco Talos, Jan 2019**: multiple CVEs (incl. CVE-2018-4032) — helper-protocol functions such as `moveItemAtPath` could be called by **any application** and ran as root, with no validation of the caller; passing `nil` as destination deleted the file. Affected v4.04; fixed in 4.2.0 — [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/vulnerability-spotlight-cleanmymac-x/); [Tenable CVE-2018-4032](https://www.tenable.com/cve/CVE-2018-4032)
- **CleanMyMac X, CVE-2019-5011 (Mar 2019)**: the update to 4.20 **failed to remove the old vulnerable helper**, leaving users exploitable after patching — [Cisco Talos](https://blog.talosintelligence.com/cleanmymacx-helper-service-remote-code-execution-vuln/)
- **Zoom for macOS auto-updater (Patrick Wardle, DEF CON 30, Aug 2022)**: CVE-2022-22781 (downgrade attack; patch took 4+ months), CVE-2022-28751 (package signature validation; 7+ months), CVE-2022-28756 (CVSS 8.8; verified package remained world-writable so it could be swapped after verification — a TOCTOU; fixed in 5.11.5) — [DEF CON 30 slides (PDF)](https://media.defcon.org/DEF%20CON%2030/DEF%20CON%2030%20presentations/Patrick%20Wardle%20-%20Youre%20-%20Muted%20-%20Rooted.pdf); [CVE-2022-28756](https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2022-28756); [The Verge, 12 Aug 2022](https://www.theverge.com/2022/8/12/23303411/zoom-defcon-root-access-privilege-escalation-hack-patrick-wardle)
- **Proxyman, CVE-2019-20057** and the general critique of Apple's EvenBetterAuthorizationSample pattern: any client can connect to the helper and pass an empty authorization reference — [Csaba Fitzl, 12 Jan 2020](https://theevilbit.github.io/posts/secure_coding_xpc_part1/)
- **Fitzl's hardening checklist**: in `shouldAcceptNewConnection` verify the client is signed by your Team ID and bundle ID and meets a minimum version; identify the client by **audit token, not PID** (PID reuse attacks); the client must use hardened runtime/library validation and must not carry `allow-dyld-environment-variables`, `disable-library-validation` or `get-task-allow` entitlements — [Csaba Fitzl](https://theevilbit.github.io/posts/secure_coding_xpc_part1/)
- **Twitch Studio helper (June 2024)**: no XPC checks; a move-file-as-root method allowed overwriting the helper itself and gaining root. The helper **remains on disk after the app is uninstalled** and after the vendor discontinued the product. The write-up notes Apple has not documented how to implement XPC securely — [Iru (formerly Kandji) research](https://www.iru.com/blog/twitch-privileged-helper)
- **".localized" installer LPE (Dec 2025)**: if a different app with the same name already exists, macOS installs into `/Applications/Name.localized/`, letting an attacker pre-plant an app at the expected path so that the vendor's LaunchDaemon runs an attacker-controlled binary as root. Affects third-party installers that run privileged code from inside the app bundle (NordVPN and Microsoft Teams installers cited). Recommendation: place privileged binaries under `/Library/...` (e.g. `/Library/PrivilegedHelperTools`) rather than inside the app bundle, and verify the install location — [Csaba Fitzl, 5 Dec 2025](https://theevilbit.github.io/posts/localized/)
- **Wardle, "Death by 1000 Installers" (DEF CON 25, 2017)**: installers/updaters routinely execute user-writable binaries as root with no validation — [DEF CON 25 slides (PDF)](https://media.defcon.org/DEF%20CON%2025/DEF%20CON%2025%20presentations/DEF%20CON%2025%20-%20Patrick-Wardle-Death-by-1000-Installers-on-MacOS-UPDATED.pdf)
- Auditors' design observations (Mullvad): keep the root daemon as small as possible, keep everything it touches in root-owned or SIP-protected paths, install via .pkg as root so the bundle isn't user-writable, and treat an auto-updater as added attack surface — [Cure53 report 2020 (PDF)](https://cure53.de/pentest-report_mullvad_2020_v2.pdf)

### Inferences
- Lessons as requirements: (1) helper API must expose **narrow, typed, intent-level operations** ("disable launch item with this label after re-validating it") and never generic path/command primitives; (2) re-validate every path inside the helper (ownership, no symlinks, `O_NOFOLLOW`, operate on file descriptors) — never trust the app's earlier check; (3) authenticate the XPC peer by audit token + code-signing requirement (Team ID, bundle ID, minimum version, hardened runtime); (4) updater: verify signature, then install from a root-owned location, block downgrades; (5) upgrades and uninstall must remove old helpers and LaunchDaemon plists; (6) privileged binaries live outside the user-modifiable bundle path.
- Each such bug in a *security* product is also a CRA reportable event if actively exploited, a PLD "defect" candidate after Dec 2026, and a reputational event disproportionate to its severity — which is the argument for auditing the helper before launch rather than after.
- On modern macOS the helper should be registered with `SMAppService` (macOS 13+) rather than legacy `SMJobBless`; this is general platform knowledge and was not sourced in this session.

### Gaps
- **MacKeeper** helper/RCE CVEs (e.g. the 2015 URL-handler RCE and the Dec 2015 exposure of ~13M user records) and **Parallels Desktop** privilege-escalation CVEs (2025) were requested but no primary source was retrieved in this session — do not cite specifics without verification.
- No source gathered on Apple's current official guidance for secure XPC peer validation (`setCodeSigningRequirement:`, macOS 13+) or `SMAppService` security properties.
- No data on how often helper vulnerabilities in consumer Mac utilities are exploited in the wild.

## 6. QA approach for detection quality: test corpus, false-positive measurement, beta programs, staged rollouts

### Takeaway
Public material is sufficient to build a labelled macOS persistence corpus (Objective-See samples + Atomic Red Team macOS atomics mapped to MITRE ATT&CK), but the commercially decisive metric is the **false-positive rate on clean Macs running real developer/enterprise software**, since launch agents/daemons are overwhelmingly used by legitimate software. Developer ID apps get beta channels and 7-group phased rollouts from Sparkle 2 out of the box.

### Cited Findings
- **Objective-See Mac Malware Collection**: public repository of live macOS malware samples (password-protected archives; also mirrored on GitHub) — [Objective-See](https://objective-see.org/malware.html)
- **Annual "Mac Malware of 20xx" reports** (8th edition covered 2023) document each new family's infection vector, **persistence mechanism** and capabilities, with sample download links — [Objective-See, The Mac Malware of 2023 (PDF)](https://objective-see.org/downloads/MacMalware_2023.pdf)
- **The Art of Mac Malware** (Patrick Wardle), free online; the persistence chapter notes that in the 2019 report every analysed persistent specimen used a launch item, and describes the key plist fields (`Label`, `Program`/`ProgramArguments`, `RunAtLoad`) and the three third-party locations — [TAOMM vol. 1, ch. 2 (PDF)](https://taomm.org/vol1/pdfs/CH%202%20Persistence.pdf)
- **MITRE ATT&CK**: T1543.001 Launch Agent and T1543.004 Launch Daemon, with real-world users (AppleJeus/3CX supply-chain attack, APT38) — [T1543.001](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1543/001/); [T1543.004](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1543/004/). ATT&CK itself states that "Launch Agents are often installed to perform updates to programs, launch user specified programs at login, or to conduct other developer tasks" — [T1543.001](https://attack.mitre.org/techniques/T1543/001/)
- **Atomic Red Team** provides executable macOS tests with cleanup commands, e.g. T1543.001 has three atomics (Launch Agent, Event Monitor Daemon (emond) persistence, Launch Agent in root directory); last updated 20 Oct 2026 — [Atomic Red Team T1543.001](https://www.atomicredteam.io/docs/atomics/T1543.001)
- **How labs do false-positive testing on macOS**: AV-TEST's FP set uses static and dynamic samples "used by average consumers" from the Mac App Store and third-party sources, on physical Mac minis with the latest macOS build; only validated clean/malicious samples are used — [AV-TEST macOS test plan 2026 (PDF)](https://www.amtso.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/AMTSO-Test-Plan-AV-TEST-MacOS_Consumer_Testing_2026_AMTSO.pdf)
- Why FP matters commercially: false alarms damage both users and the flagged developer; worst case is an unusable system — [AV-Comparatives](https://av-comparatives.org/spotlight-on-security-the-problem-with-false-alarms/)
- **Sparkle 2** (open source, MIT): `sparkle:channel` for beta channels (opt-in per updater); `sparkle:phasedRolloutInterval` rolls an update out over **7 fixed groups** (e.g. 86,400 s = one group per day, complete in 7 days); phased rollout is bypassed for critical updates and manual checks; `criticalUpdate` tag; EdDSA (ed25519) signatures on update archives — [Sparkle, Publishing an update](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/publishing/); [Sparkle documentation](https://sparkle-project.org/documentation/); [Sparkle on GitHub](https://www.github.com/sparkle-project/Sparkle)
- Evidence that rollout discipline is legally relevant: Delta's surviving gross-negligence claim pleads deployment "without staged deployments" and "without minimal testing" — [CRN](https://www.crn.com/news/security/2025/5-things-to-watch-in-delta-s-lawsuit-against-crowdstrike). Microsoft's 2023 incident only harmed machines in "block" mode, not "audit" mode — [Microsoft Q&A](https://github.com/microsoft/MDE-PowerBI-Templates/blob/refs/heads/master/ASR_scripts/ASR_rule_Block_Win32_API_calls_from_Office_Macro_issue_Q&A.md)

### Inferences
- Corpus design: **malicious set** = Objective-See samples detonated in disposable VMs (snapshot the persistence artefacts — plists, login items, cron, profiles — rather than shipping live malware in CI) + all macOS persistence atomics from Atomic Red Team; **benign set** = fresh macOS installs of each supported version plus "persona" images (developer: Homebrew, Docker, JetBrains, VS Code, Xcode tools; enterprise: MDM agent, EDR, VPN, Zoom/Teams/Slack, Microsoft AutoUpdate, Google/Adobe updaters; creative; gamer). Track per-rule FP rate; any rule that fires on the benign set cannot have an automated remediation.
- Treat **rule/definition updates as code**: same staged rollout, canary group, kill-switch and rollback as binaries — the CrowdStrike, McAfee, Webroot and Defender incidents were all *content/signature* updates, not application releases.
- Release gates worth adopting: every remediation has a tested undo; a "dry-run/audit" default; a hard-coded protected list (Apple-signed, SIP paths, the product's own components — Sophos detected its own updater); telemetry-free FP reporting button so users can report a wrong verdict.
- Beta programme for a Developer ID app: Sparkle beta channel + a public/opt-in tester list; consider starting with read-only (no remediation) builds for the widest cohort.
- Note a trade-off flagged by auditors: an auto-updater is itself attack surface (section 5), so the Sparkle integration belongs in audit scope.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from an Apple primary source in this session that **TestFlight for macOS is limited to apps distributed via App Store Connect** (the Apple documentation page returned no content); the premise in the brief is consistent with general knowledge but is unsourced here.
- No count of total macOS-tagged Atomic Red Team tests or of the full ATT&CK macOS persistence technique list was retrieved (only T1543.001/.004 pages).
- No public benchmark exists (that I found) for an acceptable false-positive rate for persistence scanners; AV-TEST/AV-Comparatives FP numbers relate to file-scanning AV.

## 7. Business/legal setup for a Brazil-based solo seller: entity, merchant of record, trademark, ToS/privacy/DPA, insurance, support and incident response (LGPD/GDPR)

### Takeaway
MEI is not legally available for software development/licensing; the standard vehicle is a **Sociedade Limitada Unipessoal (SLU) in Simples Nacional**, which also provides the limited liability this risk profile needs. A merchant of record removes tax/chargeback/invoicing burden but **not** product liability, warranty, privacy or support obligations. LGPD requires breach notification in 3 business days (doubled for small agents); GDPR in 72 hours; the CRA adds 24 h/72 h vulnerability reporting. The product name needs trademark clearance, including against Apple's rules for names containing "Mac".

### Cited Findings

**Entity and tax (Brazil)**
- Software development/licensing is not among MEI-permitted occupations (Annex XI, Resolução CGSN 140/2018; none of the CNAE division 62 subclasses — incl. 6202-3/00 and 6203-1/00 licensing — are listed). PLP 25/2026, which would add it, was still pending in the Chamber as of 2 Nov 2026 — [Mistrello Assessoria, 2 Nov 2026 (accounting firm; secondary)](https://mistrelloassessoria.com.br/insights/programador-pode-ser-mei/); official statement that MEI may only perform Annex XI occupations and has an R$81,000 annual limit — [gov.br DREI FAQ](https://www.gov.br/empresas-e-negocios/pt-br/drei/arquivos/faq/perguntas-frequentes)
- MEI ceiling reportedly rises to R$110,000 in 2027 and R$140,000 from 2028 under LC 238/2026, without changing the occupation list (single secondary source — verify) — [Mistrello Assessoria](https://mistrelloassessoria.com.br/insights/programador-pode-ser-mei/)
- EI vs SLU: an Empresário Individual has **no separation** between personal and business assets; an SLU limits liability to the quotas (Civil Code art. 1.052), with no minimum capital. Microempresa up to R$360,000/yr; EPP up to R$4.8M/yr — [Mistrello Assessoria](https://mistrelloassessoria.com.br/insights/programador-pode-ser-mei/); [RBO Contabilidade, 30 Sep 2026](https://rbocontabilidade.com/contabilidade-para-desenvolvedores/)
- SLU opening cost R$500–1,500 and up to ~5 business days (Apr 2026, accounting-software vendor) — [Simplifique/Contmatic](https://simplifique.contmatic.com.br/blogs/pj-para-desenvolvedor-como-abrir-gerir-empresa-2026)
- Simples Nacional for software: Annex III from 6% if payroll incl. pró-labore ≥ 28% of 12-month revenue ("Fator R"), otherwise Annex V from 15.5% — [RBO Contabilidade](https://rbocontabilidade.com/contabilidade-para-desenvolvedores/); [Agilize, 20 Feb 2026](https://agilize.com.br/artigos/tributacao-desenvolvedor-software-simples-nacional/)
- Export of services under Simples: revenue segregated in PGDAS-D does not pay the PIS, Cofins and ISS portions of the DAS, but IRPJ, CSLL and CPP remain. Three cumulative conditions: customer resident abroad, payment represents inflow of foreign currency, and the result of the service is not verified in Brazil. There is an additional R$4.8M ceiling for export revenue — [RBO Contabilidade](https://rbocontabilidade.com/contabilidade-para-desenvolvedores/)
- From Jan 2026, profits above R$50,000/month paid by one company to the same partner suffer 10% income-tax withholding (Lei 15.270/2025) — [RBO Contabilidade](https://rbocontabilidade.com/contabilidade-para-desenvolvedores/)
- Tax reform: from 1 Jan 2027 the DAS replaces PIS/Cofins with CBS/IBS; starting rates unchanged (6% / 15.5%) — [Mistrello Assessoria](https://mistrelloassessoria.com.br/insights/programador-pode-ser-mei/)

**Merchant of record (MoR)**
- An MoR is the legal reseller: it owes VAT/sales tax, appears on the card statement, handles invoicing, fraud screening and chargebacks — [Paddle seller handbook](https://www.paddle.com/seller-guides/seller-handbook); [Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/merchant-of-record)
- The software itself is still **licensed by the supplier under the supplier's EULA** (Lemon Squeezy buyer terms) — [Lemon Squeezy buyer terms](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/buyer-terms?referrer=wordpress.com)
- Paddle Master Services Agreement: fee 5% + US$0.50 per checkout transaction; Paddle may refund buyers within 14 days at its discretion; the supplier bears refunds, chargebacks and a fee of up to 20 USD/EUR/GBP per dispute; Paddle may reject or suspend any product that falls outside its Acceptable Use Policy or "risk tolerance" and may suspend/terminate for excessive chargeback or refund rates; supplier indemnifies Paddle for tax errors caused by wrong product information — [Paddle terms](https://www.paddle.com/legal/terms)
- Dispute-rate expectations: average 0.1–0.3% of transactions; above 0.75% "unacceptable" — [Paddle seller handbook](https://www.paddle.com/seller-guides/seller-handbook)
- Fee comparison (2026): Paddle 5% + $0.50; Lemon Squeezy 5% + $0.50 (+1.5% international), acquired by Stripe in 2024; FastSpring 5–8% custom. Lost chargebacks are passed back to the seller under all of them — [Chargeflow](https://www.chargeflow.io/blog/merchant-of-record); [tiun (competitor MoR; interested party)](https://tiun.io/blog/merchant-of-record-chargeback-liability)

**Trademark / product name**
- "Mac", "macOS", "MacBook" etc. are registered Apple trademarks — [Apple Trademark List](https://www.apple.com/legal/intellectual-property/trademark/appletmlist.html)
- Apple's third-party guidelines (text retrieved from an **unofficial copy**; verify against Apple's official "Guidelines for Using Apple Trademarks and Copyrights" page): Apple marks may not be used in a product/company name except as noted; **"Mac" may be used in a product name** if the product is not a computer or OS, is Mac-compatible, "Mac" is combined with "another non-generic or non-geographically-descriptive word" (examples given: acceptable "MacVenus", "MacCharlie"; not acceptable "MacCharleston", "MacSales"), "Mac" is not more prominent than the rest of the name, the name suggests no association with Apple, and the user acknowledges Apple's ownership of "Mac" — [Unofficial copy of Apple trademark guidelines (PDF)](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/d37a9b24-bc42-4cb1-ab3b-3d1b21b01aec/downloads/29597475605.pdf)

**Data protection and incident response**
- LGPD / Resolução CD/ANPD 15/2024 (in force since 26 Apr 2024): the controller must notify ANPD **and** affected data subjects within **3 business days** of learning that an incident affected personal data, when it may cause relevant risk or damage (criteria include authentication data, financial data, sensitive data, large scale); complementary information within 20 business days; **deadlines are doubled for small-scale processing agents** (Resolução CD/ANPD 2/2022); incident records must be kept at least 5 years; non-compliance can trigger sanctioning proceedings — [Diário Oficial da União, Resolução CD/ANPD 15/2024](http://in.gov.br/en/web/dou/-/resolucao-cd/anpd-n-15-de-24-de-abril-de-2024-556243024); [Machado Meyer, 30 Apr 2024](https://www.machadomeyer.com.br/pt/inteligencia-juridica/publicacoes-ij/direito-digital/anpd-publica-regulamento-de-comunicacao-de-incidente-de-seguranca); [Riskflow](https://riskflow.com.br/guias/comunicacao-incidente-anpd-prazo)
- International transfers relying on contractual clauses must use ANPD's standard clauses (Resolução CD/ANPD 19/2024; adaptation deadline ended Aug 2025) — [Merovian, 15 Oct 2026](https://merovian.app/blog/contratos-saas-clausulas-essenciais)
- GDPR Art. 27 EU-representative duty for non-EU sellers (see section 2) — [GDPR Art. 27](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/article/27/adopted?view=plain)
- CRA reporting duties 24 h / 72 h / 14 days since 11 Sep 2026 (see section 2) — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting)

**Insurance (Brazil; broker marketing pages, mostly undated — indicative only)**
- Tech E&O (RC Profissional para tecnologia) covers bugs, failed implementations and SLA breaches; cyber covers security incidents, ransomware, data leaks, LGPD costs; they are complementary and "one does not replace the other". Policies are claims-made with retroactivity and extended reporting options — [NeuCorr](https://www.neucorr.com/seguros/responsabilidade-civil/responsabilidade-civil-profissional-tecnologia); [GVM Consulting](https://gvmconsulting.com.br/seguros/responsabilidade-civil-empresas-de-tecnologia/)
- Tech E&O premium: R$2,500–40,000/yr depending on revenue, clients, scope and limit — [Vivax Corretora](https://www.vivaxcorretora.com/rc-profissional-empresas-tecnologia)
- E&O reference rates: limit up to R$500k at 1.2–2.5%/yr ≈ R$6,000–12,500; R$500k–1M ≈ R$10,000–20,000; technology tends to price above average. Since Dec 2025, Lei 15.040/2024 requires the defence-cost limit to be separate from the indemnity limit — [BrSeguro](https://www.brseguro.com/seguro-e-o-erros-e-omissoes)
- Early-stage startup pack (E&O + cyber + D&O): R$8,000–25,000/yr — [BrSeguro startups](https://www.brseguro.com/seguro-para-startups-tecnologia-e-inovacao); combined property + E&O + cyber R$3,000–60,000/yr — [Vivax](https://www.vivaxcorretora.com/seguro-empresarial-empresa-tecnologia)
- Cyber: average premium R$20,000–40,000/yr (broker e-book, Jan 2025; skewed to larger firms) — [Innoa Seguros (PDF)](https://innoaseguros.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Ebook-Manual-Seguro-Cyber.pdf); in 2025 rates fell ~10% on renewal and SMEs now see rates below 1% of the limit with lighter underwriting — [TI Inside / Howden Brasil, 24 Nov 2025](https://tiinside.com.br/my/24/11/2025/brasil-segue-tendencia-global-de-inflexao-nos-precos-de-seguros-ciberneticos/)
- Underwriters ask whether the insured operates outside Brazil and whether contracts contain limitation-of-liability clauses — [NeuCorr](https://www.neucorr.com/seguros/responsabilidade-civil/responsabilidade-civil-profissional-tecnologia)

### Inferences
- Entity: SLU (not EI, not MEI) is the minimum sensible structure given strict consumer liability exposure; limited liability is not absolute in Brazilian consumer cases (disregard of legal entity under the CDC is comparatively easy) — this specific point is unsourced here and needs counsel. **[LAWYER-BR]**
- MoR and export tax treatment interact: with an MoR the Brazilian company's contractual customer is the MoR (a foreign legal entity) paying in foreign currency, which simplifies the "customer abroad + foreign-currency inflow" tests even for sales to end users located in Brazil — but whether sales whose end user is in Brazil satisfy the "result not verified in Brazil" test is a classic dispute. **[LAWYER-BR / accountant]**
- MoR ≠ liability shield: product defects, data loss, privacy and support stay with the vendor; in addition, the MoR's AUP/risk tolerance is a **platform risk** for a "cleaner/security utility" category with a scareware history — pre-clear the product with the MoR before building the checkout, and keep refund/chargeback rates low via a generous refund policy.
- Naming: "Mac Doctor"-style names combine "Mac" with a common/descriptive word; under the guideline's "non-generic word" criterion and the prominence rule this is at least arguable, and names in this space are crowded (prior "Mac…Doctor/Cleaner/Keeper" products). Run INPI, USPTO and EUIPO searches and consider a distinctive coined name with "for Mac" as a descriptor. **[LAWYER-BR] [LAWYER-US/EU IP]**
- Document set implied by the findings: EULA (with consumer-law carve-outs and CDC-compliant highlighted limitations), Terms of Sale aligned with the MoR's terms, Privacy Policy covering LGPD + GDPR (controller identity, legal bases, transfers, representative), a DPA template only if business customers send personal data to the vendor (a local-only tool with no telemetry minimises this), a published VDP/security.txt, a support-period statement (CRA, from Dec 2027), and an incident runbook with three clocks: CRA 24 h/72 h, GDPR 72 h, LGPD 3 business days (6 if qualifying as small agent).
- Privacy-by-architecture is the cheapest compliance: on-device analysis, no account, no telemetry by default, and scan reports that never leave the Mac reduce LGPD/GDPR scope to payment/support data largely held by the MoR. Avast's US$16.5M order is the cautionary tale for monetising security-product data.
- Insurance: expect roughly R$6,000–25,000/yr for an entry-level E&O + cyber combination, but confirm (a) worldwide territorial scope **including US/Canada jurisdiction**, which is commonly excluded or surcharged, (b) coverage for consumer B2C software (most Brazilian tech E&O wording assumes B2B service contracts), and (c) retroactive date. Quotes required.
- Support expectations (inference from the PUP and FTC material rather than a cited standard): reachable contact identity in the EULA, fast refunds, clear cancellation, and a documented path to undo any remediation are what keep chargebacks, PUP listings and regulator attention away.
